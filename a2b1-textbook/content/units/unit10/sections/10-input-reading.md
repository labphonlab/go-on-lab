Leo went to a new city for the first time. He didn't know the way to the station, so he stopped a woman on the street.

"Excuse me," he said. "Could you tell me how to get to the station?"

The woman smiled and said, "Sure! Go straight and turn left at the corner. It's next to the bank."

Leo thanked her and walked that way.

Later, Leo felt hungry, so he found a small restaurant near the station. A waiter came to his table.

"Would you like to order?" the waiter asked.

"Yes, could I have the chicken with rice, please? And could I have some water, too?" Leo said.

"Of course," the waiter said.

While Leo ate his meal, he started to feel sick. He had a headache and a sore throat. He asked the waiter, "Is there a pharmacy near here?"

"Yes, there's one across from the station," the waiter said. "You should go soon."

At the pharmacy, the pharmacist, a friendly woman who worked there every day, gave him some medicine.

"You shouldn't go out in the cold tonight," she said. "You should rest and drink a lot of water."

The next day, Leo felt much better. He met a classmate from his old school — the same person who used to sit next to him in class!

They talked about their trip, and Leo told her about the kind woman who gave him directions, the polite waiter from the restaurant, and the pharmacist who gave him medicine and told him what to do.

It was a busy day, but a good one.
