About this atlas

A critical companion to Ladefoged & Maddieson's The Sounds of the World's Languages, built on PHOIBLE 2.0 and Glottolog.

Why parallel display?

Phoneme-inventory databases routinely merge several descriptions of the same language into a single inventory, and replace contrastive phonemes with characteristic allophones. Both moves destroy the abstract phonological system they claim to record (Simpson 1999), and when a database integrates sources it hides the inconsistencies between them (Hammarström et al. 2023).

This atlas takes the opposite stance. Its design principles are:

  • Never merge. Multiple inventories of one language are shown side by side, each labelled with its source.
  • Keep provenance. Every segment is traceable to the inventory and source it came from.
  • Show the disagreement. Colour coding describes where sources agree or differ; it never edits the underlying data.
  • State the limits. This is a convenience sample.

Coverage & limitations

English (stan1293) is a telling example: its nine inventories name 94 distinct segments between them, yet only 21 appear in all nine. A merged database would report one number and erase that disagreement.
